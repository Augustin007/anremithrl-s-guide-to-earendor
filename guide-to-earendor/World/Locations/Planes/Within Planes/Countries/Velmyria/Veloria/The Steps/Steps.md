The Steps are named so because of the local geography. Natural processes with the constant blizzards and ice formed enormous smooth cliffs in step-like formations. Mostly natives of Veloria live here, as opposed to Velorians. Despite there being different rulers and cultures and religions, their borders aren't well-defined. Stepfolk, as they are called by Velorians, have little care for international politics beyond their own safety. Many of the groups here were not originally from the steps but from further south, driven north by Velorian expansionism. 

It snows much of the year, here, and settlements only survive through deep knowledge of the cold, living in basins and small areas protects on shelves of the Steps.

The sea is as central to the Steps as the cliffs. Most settlements cling to the coast, living by fish, seals, whale-hunting, kelp-gathering, salt, cold-water trade, and salvage. Inland communities exist, especially in sheltered valleys and mining terraces, but they are fewer and more isolated. 

Borders are famously indistinct. A Velorian diplomat might demand to know whether a valley belongs to Kalean or Tlindi, only to be told that it belongs to the families who winter there, except during the reindeer migration, when the upper grazing rights pass to another clan, unless the shrine at the pass has declared a mourning season, in which case no one may camp there at all. This is not disorder to the Stepfolk. It is law, but law of a kind built from use, memory, kinship, and survival rather than from cadastral surveys.

While the Paros is accepted here, on occasion, it is not really the central currency, which is mostly just trades and agreements. 

Religion in the Steps is extremely local. The great gods are known, but they are often worshipped through northern titles, household rites, and syncretic customs that make southern priests uncomfortable. 

The central moral unit is often the hearth. To refuse shelter in killing weather is among the gravest sins in many Step cultures. 

The Steps were never fully part of Veloria, though Veloria has long regarded them as an obvious northern possession. Their harbours, fisheries, mineral shelves, and strategic position make them attractive; their poverty, weather, and resistance make them costly. Veloria would likely have absorbed them, had international treaties not checked Velorian expansion. This has left the Steps in an uneasy position: formally independent, economically pressured, and perpetually mapped by people who assume independence is a temporary inconvenience.

Mythnor is the most diplomatically visible of the Step realms, largely because its southern harbours are the easiest for Velorian ships to reach. Its rulers style themselves kings, though their authority is strongest on the coast and weakens rapidly inland. Mythnor is the most accustomed to foreign trade and the most divided over it. Its harbour-lords grow wealthy by dealing with Veloria, while its highland clans accuse them of selling the Steps one pier at a time.

Lyalase is colder, poorer, and more inward-looking. Its settlements are scattered along sheltered inlets and high terraces where survival depends on strict communal discipline. Lyalasi law is famous for its severity and its harsh punishments for lawbreakers.

Shemliedil is a land of old shrines, storm rites, and cliff monasteries. It has no single king in the ordinary sense, but a network of sacred houses whose authority rises in times of crisis. Shemliedil is also where older native traditions remain strongest.

Kalean is the most martial of the Step polities. Its people control several important passes and have long served as mercenaries, guides, and winter fighters. Kalean warbands are not large, but still very compentent and respected.

Tlindi does not touch the coast. It is a confederation of inland valleys, reindeer routes, and upper terraces. Its people are semi-nomadic, moving with herds and seasonal hunting patterns. Tlindi has almost no use for fixed borders, and this has caused repeated disputes with its neighbours.

Tinas is a narrow coastal country of fishers, shipwrights, and wreck-law courts. Its towns cling beneath the cliffs, protected by breakwaters and old sea-walls. Tinasi are famous for their boats, which are small, light, swift, and well-built,.

Imyelian lies among the higher snowfields and is one of the least accessible Step realms. The Imyelians speak little, trade fairly, and remember insults for generations.

Yenor is a blip on the map. The coldest part of the coast. It is a land of long winters. Yenor is ruled by exiles of Shemliedil, and most people who live there were once born in Shemliedil. It is the land of punishment, where exiles live.
